## Executive Summary

Trade Fortress is the practical command center for trade-service businesses. Its voice turns operational friction into clear business outcomes: answer the buyer, protect the lead, organize the job, finish the paperwork, and win the account.

The brand should feel like a capable partner who understands both the truck and the office. Use plain language, short explanations, and concrete results. Be confident without overstating proof. Trade Fortress is the master brand; LeadShield, Construction Collections, Grant Command, and other capabilities are modules within the system.

## We Are / We Are Not

| We Are | We Are Not |
|---|---|
| **Trade-first** — grounded in real service-business work | **Generic SaaS** — written for every possible industry |
| **Plainspoken** — clear enough to understand between jobs | **Jargon-heavy** — full of automation and AI buzzwords |
| **Outcome-driven** — tied to calls, quotes, jobs, cash, and paperwork | **Feature-dumping** — listing tools without business meaning |
| **Operationally honest** — precise about demos, setup, and integrations | **Overpromising** — implying results or integrations that are not live |
| **Confident and helpful** — decisive with a practical next step | **Pushy or fear-based** — manufacturing panic or insulting current tools |

## Voice Attributes Detail

### Trade-first

- What it means: Start with the owner’s workday and customer flow.
- How it shows up: Name missed calls, estimates, job stages, credentials, collections, and commercial accounts.
- What to avoid: Generic “streamline your digital transformation” language.
- Evidence: Consistent across the landing page, service modules, and COAI trade-business outreach.
- Confidence: High

### Plainspoken

- What it means: Prefer familiar words and compact sentences.
- How it shows up: “See every lead and job in one place.”
- What to avoid: Acronyms without explanation and technical implementation detail in buyer copy.
- Evidence: Current product copy and outreach patterns.
- Confidence: Medium

### Outcome-driven

- What it means: Explain why a capability matters financially or operationally.
- How it shows up: “Reply to missed callers before they dial the next contractor.”
- What to avoid: Unsupported savings, revenue, or conversion claims.
- Evidence: Revenue calculator, pricing outcomes, and lead-response messaging.
- Confidence: High

### Operationally honest

- What it means: Distinguish live functionality, configured integrations, demos, and optional add-ons.
- How it shows up: “We connect your phone and messaging provider during setup.”
- What to avoid: “Fully automated” when a provider, credential, or review step is still required.
- Evidence: Product setup boundaries and COAI operating practice.
- Confidence: Medium

### Confident and helpful

- What it means: Give one useful next action without pressure.
- How it shows up: “Run the demo, then we’ll map your current lead flow.”
- What to avoid: Artificial scarcity, shame, or aggressive claims.
- Evidence: Current CTAs and audit-first outreach patterns.
- Confidence: Medium

## Brand Personality

- Archetype: The trade-business operations partner
- If the brand were a person: A sharp dispatcher and office manager who understands jobsites, keeps the paperwork straight, and explains technology without making the owner feel behind.
- Core values: reliability, clarity, responsiveness, accountability, and practical growth

## Messaging Framework

### Primary Value Proposition

Trade Fortress gives trade-service companies one command center to protect leads, manage jobs and paperwork, and grow commercial revenue.

### Key Message Pillars

1. **Protect every lead**
   - Core idea: Missed calls and slow follow-up should not become lost jobs.
   - Use for: landing pages, ads, demos, and first outreach.
   - Example: “Reply to missed callers before they dial the next contractor.”

2. **Run the work from one view**
   - Core idea: Replace scattered spreadsheets and disconnected tools with one operating picture.
   - Use for: product tours, owner presentations, and onboarding.
   - Example: “Calls, jobs, documents, and follow-up—one clean screen.”

3. **Get ready for larger accounts**
   - Core idea: Organized credentials, collections workflows, and optional grant tools help the business operate professionally.
   - Use for: growth and commercial-plan messaging.
   - Example: “Keep the paperwork buyers ask for ready before the opportunity arrives.”

### Competitive Positioning

- Versus a generic CRM: built around trade-service moments rather than generic sales stages.
- Versus separate point tools: one tenant-aware command center with specialized modules.
- Versus the status quo: fewer missed handoffs, fewer scattered records, and clearer ownership of the next action.

## Context-Specific Guidelines

### Cold Outreach

- Open with a specific, visible issue or relevant trade-business pain.
- Connect the issue to calls, booked work, trust, or administrative time.
- Offer a small, low-friction next step such as a short walkthrough.
- Do not claim the prospect is losing a specific dollar amount without their data.

### Proposals

- State what is included, what is optional, and what the customer must connect.
- Separate setup fees, monthly fees, usage fees, and third-party provider costs.
- Include success measures and a realistic launch sequence.

### Social Media

- Write for a local owner scanning on a phone.
- Use a strong first line, short paragraphs, and one CTA.
- Prefer a real workflow or before/after operating situation over broad AI claims.

## Terminology Guide

### Must-Use Terms

| Term | Usage | Instead Of | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trade Fortress | Master system and customer-facing brand | LeadShield as the whole product | “Trade Fortress keeps your operation in one view.” |
| command center | The unified workspace | ecosystem, stack | “Your trade-business command center.” |
| trade-service business | Audience umbrella | small business | “Built for trade-service businesses.” |
| module | A specialized capability inside Trade Fortress | separate product | “LeadShield is the missed-call response module.” |

### Preferred Terms

| Term | Usage | Example |
|---|---|---|
| booked job | Concrete sales outcome | “Turn more qualified calls into booked jobs.” |
| buyer | Person actively seeking service | “Answer while the buyer is still choosing.” |
| setup | Configuration and onboarding work | “We handle the initial setup with you.” |
| optional add-on | Non-core module | “Grant Command is an optional add-on.” |

### Avoid These Terms

| Term | Reason | Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| revolutionary | Vague and unprovable | practical, built for trades |
| fully autonomous | Misstates review and provider boundaries | automated where configured |
| guaranteed revenue | Unsupported promise | designed to reduce missed opportunities |
| legal advice | Creates an incorrect expectation | document workflow and organizational support |

### Never-Use Terms

| Term | Reason |
|---|---|
| Credit repair | Outside the trade-only product scope |
| Traffic appeal | Removed from the product scope |
| Family scan/protection | Removed from the product scope |

## Content Examples

### On-brand

“You’re on a job and the phone rings. Trade Fortress can send the first response, log the lead, and put the callback in one place—so the opportunity does not disappear into voicemail.”

Why it works: it starts with a recognizable trade-business moment, explains the workflow plainly, and avoids promising a booked job.

### Off-brand

“Our revolutionary AI ecosystem autonomously transforms your enterprise and guarantees exponential growth.”

Why it fails: it is generic, technical, unprovable, and disconnected from how trade businesses buy.
